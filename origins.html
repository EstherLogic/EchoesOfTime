Historical Background

A Changing World

The attacks of September 11, 2001 took place within a much larger historical context. During the decades before 2001, international relations changed significantly. The end of the Cold War created a new political environment, while conflicts in different parts of the Middle East influenced international politics and security.

During the 1990s, terrorist organizations increasingly targeted American interests in different parts of the world. Several attacks and warnings demonstrated that international terrorism had become an important security concern.

Al-Qaeda, an extremist terrorist organization led by Osama bin Laden, developed an increasingly hostile relationship with the United States. The organization carried out attacks against American targets during the 1990s and eventually planned an operation on American soil.

Intelligence agencies received information about potential threats before September 2001. However, the full scale and specific nature of the planned attack were not understood in time to prevent the events that followed.

The World Trade Center

A Symbol of New York

The World Trade Center was a large complex of buildings in Lower Manhattan, New York City. Its Twin Towers were among the most recognizable structures in the world and became an important part of the city's skyline.

The complex was designed to support international business and trade. Thousands of people worked in the buildings every day, while visitors came from many different countries.

Why was the World Trade Center important?

The World Trade Center was an important center of international business and was one of the most recognizable landmarks in New York City.

What happened before September 11?

During the 1990s, several terrorist attacks targeted American interests. These events contributed to growing concerns about international terrorism.

Why is the event historically significant?

September 11 had a major impact on American history and international affairs. Its consequences continued to shape politics, security and public memory for many years.
