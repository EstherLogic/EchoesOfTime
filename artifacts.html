The Historical Collection

Objects That Preserve Memory

Historical events leave behind more than written records. Buildings, personal belongings, uniforms, documents, photographs and other physical objects can become part of the historical record.

After September 11, many objects connected with the World Trade Center and the people affected by the attacks were preserved. Some objects became part of museum collections, while others were kept by families or organizations.

These objects help historians understand the scale of the event while also reminding visitors that history is made up of individual experiences. A simple object can sometimes provide a connection between the present and a moment in the past.

What Can Objects Tell Us?

History Beyond the Written Record

Objects can provide historians with another way to study the past. Written documents tell us what people recorded, while physical objects can show what existed in a particular place and time.

In the case of September 11, preserved objects became part of a much larger historical archive. They help museums and researchers document the World Trade Center, the emergency response and the experiences of people affected by the event.

The importance of an artifact does not always come from its size or appearance. Sometimes an ordinary object becomes historically important because of the story connected to it. A photograph, piece of clothing, document or personal belonging can represent a much larger human story.

Museums carefully preserve these materials so that future generations can study them. Instead of allowing historical objects to disappear, museums create collections that allow visitors to encounter evidence from the past and learn about its significance.

A Digital Museum

Preserving the Past for the Future

A museum collection is more than a group of objects. Each artifact is selected, documented and placed within a larger historical context. Together, these materials create a record that can be studied by students, historians and visitors.

Digital museums add another layer to this process. A person can explore photographs and historical objects from almost anywhere in the world. This makes historical education more accessible and allows important memories to reach new generations.

Why are artifacts important?

Artifacts provide physical evidence from the past. They can help historians understand people, places and events beyond what can be learned from written sources alone.

Where can historical objects be preserved?

Historical objects can be preserved in museums, archives, memorial collections and private collections. Professional preservation helps protect them for future research.

Why do museums collect personal objects?

Personal objects can help tell individual stories. When they are carefully documented, they can become valuable evidence for understanding a historical event.
